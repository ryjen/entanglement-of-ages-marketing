# Book IV public visual direction — abundance under comparison

This document refines the public-safe visual direction for **Book IV — *The Age of Forms***. It is a presentation specification, not a canon source. Private manuscript text, hidden story mechanics, unpublished scene detail, and private provenance must not be copied here.

The approved public hero asset key is `age-of-forms-hero`; the production asset is manifest-backed and may be regenerated through the normal responsive-media pipeline when derivative coverage is expanded.

## Narrative job

The image should make the civilization look **materially successful** before it looks troubling.

Food, shelter, medicine, energy, fabrication, logistics, and advanced technology are broadly available. The visual tension comes from things that remain scarce even when material survival is not: attention, invitation, visibility, belonging, intimacy, prestige, and being chosen.

A viewer should be able to think, at first glance, **this looks like somewhere I might want to live**. The comparative pressure should become visible only on a second look.

## Composition

Use an inhabited public or semi-public environment with sophisticated systems integrated into ordinary life. The architecture and infrastructure should feel maintained, useful, and desirable rather than post-apocalyptic.

Several people should share the same materially comfortable environment while attention or access is distributed unevenly. Useful devices include:

- a queue, invitation boundary, gathering, service, venue, or opportunity with uneven prominence;
- a viewing platform, reflection, framed representation, or physical threshold that suggests partial evaluation;
- people moving freely while one person or small group remains peripheral without an explicit guard or coercer;
- polished inherited systems alongside ordinary repair, adaptation, plants, textiles, water, food, or human-scale civic life;
- differences in visibility and social gravity rather than differences in basic material safety.

Do not visually explain the system. Show what the environment produces.

## Relationship to the series

The image should remain recognizably part of the same cinematic series language:

- people at human scale inside larger systems;
- routes and thresholds;
- asymmetric environmental composition;
- physically plausible materials and lighting;
- controlled darkness with readable local contrast;
- no central sacred or heroic figure.

It must also be distinct from *Neurion* (Book 2). Avoid Neurion's dominant signal-cyan/network-light language. Book IV should feel later, quieter, more materially settled, and more socially comparative.

## Palette direction

Do not lock a final palette until candidate artwork is reviewed. For exploration:

- retain natural skin tones and believable materials;
- consider warm mineral, pale metallic, living green, deep charcoal, muted amber, or soft civic light against darker structural surfaces;
- use light and prominence to suggest attention, not moral superiority;
- avoid rust/brown ruin grading and avoid making the setting read as a neon cyberpunk city.

## Avoid

- floating attractiveness, reputation, or social-credit scores;
- readable UI, smartphone-feed parody, or ranking dashboards;
- influencer, gender-war, or incel visual shorthand;
- generic *Black Mirror* staging;
- generic cyberpunk or desert/post-apocalypse imagery;
- pristine utopia with no human friction;
- a single villain AI, glowing eye, machine deity, or central control room;
- heroic protagonist casting that prematurely fixes a canonical appearance;
- visible deprivation as the primary way to signal exclusion.

## Public-safe alt intent

> People share a materially comfortable later public space where advanced systems support ordinary life while attention and access gather unevenly around different people.

Final alt text must describe the approved image itself rather than this intent.

## Approval gate

Before the asset becomes deployable:

1. produce or commission a source master of at least 2400 × 1350;
2. record creator class, provenance, and rights basis;
3. review for story fit, stereotypes, unwanted religious shorthand, accidental text/logos, and spoiler leakage;
4. strip unnecessary embedded metadata;
5. approve public-safe alt text;
6. generate responsive 480/960/1440 AVIF and WebP derivatives through the existing media pipeline;
7. add source/derivatives to `public-manifest.json` with checksums and required media provenance;
8. integrate the image into Book IV and series surfaces only after approval;
9. validate responsive crops, 200% text zoom, reduced-motion/no-image behavior, and performance budgets.

Implementation and approval are tracked in issue #94.
