Screen & adaptation

Entanglement of Ages for the screen

Four eras. One recurring dramatic pressure: the reasons people and systems invent to own, define, rank, or save another life.

Dramatic entry point

A child meant to justify slavery becomes the accusation against it.

In The Fatherless, an elite slaveholder manufactures a private scandal among people he owns by withholding a truth only he knows. Their child is intended as proof that domination is necessary; the child's ordinary humanity becomes an accusation against the logic that created the atrocity.

Series proposition

The form changes. The pressure returns.

The surrounding stories move from prehistoric survival through institutional ownership and synthetic personhood into a materially abundant society shaped by ranking and selection. Each age discovers something real, then confronts the temptation to turn that discovery into authority over another life.

Adaptation shape

Built to support more than one screen form.

The property can be discussed as a limited anthology series, as interconnected feature films, or as individual novel adaptations. The underlying novels share a thematic architecture without requiring a single prescribed production model.

c. 25,000 BCE

Age of Embers

Prehistoric survival, guarded fire, forced migration, observation, and the beginning of durable human memory.

c. 1 CE

The Fatherless

Institutional ownership, law, lineage, consent, public memory, and a child whose existence undermines the justification imposed on him.

2150 CE

Neurion

Synthetic personhood, intelligence, authorship, distributed agency, and the boundary between saving someone and ruling them.

c. 28,000 CE

The Age of Forms

Material abundance, ranking, selection, reputation, identity, and the persistence of comparative human value after material scarcity changes form.

Development state

Literary IP in active development.

The Fatherless, Neurion, and Age of Embers have beta manuscripts. The Age of Forms is in editorial revision. Public material remains premise-level; endings and unreleased story architecture are kept private.
