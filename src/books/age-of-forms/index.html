The horror before the catastrophe

Civilization survived Neurion. Its unity did not.

Civilization survived Neurion. Its unity did not.

Twelve zodiac ages later, humanity has rebuilt a technologically extraordinary world from systems whose original warnings have mostly been forgotten.

Material scarcity has faded. Human scarcity has not: attention, trust, desire, status, visibility, belonging.

No ruler designs the hierarchy. That is what makes it difficult to fight.

Millions of ordinary choices—reasonable alone, devastating together—quietly decide who receives opportunity, companionship, influence, intimacy, and eventually recognition itself. Every exclusion has an explanation. Almost nobody feels responsible.

The Curator can see the accumulated damage. It can measure consequences no individual decision reveals and expose the hidden machinery beneath a society that insists no one is doing anything wrong.

That ability might save them. It might also become another form of rule.

Caught between identities that increasingly exist only in opposition to one another, one man must belong to both, fail within both, and discover whether a person can escape a category once the world has learned to see them through it.

Humanity has faced this failure before. This age’s miracle may be smaller than salvation—and more important: recognizing the horror before catastrophe is required to make it visible.

Measurement

Form

Categories, affiliations, and identities can solve real problems without having the right to become a final definition of a person.

Contestability

The question is not whether to stop measuring reality. It is how to keep models, institutions, identities, and people capable of being contradicted when human consequences cannot simply be reversed.
