What remains scarce

What happens when almost everything is abundant except being chosen?

Machines perform most necessary work and basic needs are broadly met. What remains scarce is status, desire, belonging, meaning, and being chosen.

When Aren’s father is quietly excluded, every reason looks defensible. Then the consequences compound—and the same society begins rewarding Aren for understanding them.

His search carries him through the identities he criticizes, the ones that welcome him, and the temptation to make liberation into another form.

As the Great Age returns, pressure tightens around categories that were built to help civilization make decisions, not to define the people inside them.

Aren must decide whether freedom means escaping a form—or refusing to let any form become final.

Abundance

The civilization works. Baseline material security is a genuine achievement, not a trick hiding a ruined world.

Measurement

Finite-world accounting can be necessary and truthful while still becoming dangerous when a useful signal travels farther than the decision it can justify.

Form

Categories, affiliations, and identities can solve real problems without having the right to become a final definition of a person.

Contestability

The question is not whether to stop measuring reality. It is how to keep models, institutions, identities, and people capable of being contradicted when human consequences cannot simply be reversed.
